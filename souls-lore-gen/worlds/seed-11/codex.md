# Codex of Found Things (seed 11)

*The tide gives, and the tide is given to; what remains to us are the keepings, and each was kept by a hand that loved only its own account. Read them against one another, pilgrim, for no single telling was ever meant to hold.*

## Armors

### Irric's Set

The harness of Irric of the Empty Scabbard, last-sworn of the Order. Irric went to the deep roads where the affliction is said to begin, bearing the order's final blessing — an age gone now, by the older reckonings. Word came that Irric fell to the marked. Then word came that Irric led them. The order believes neither, and keeps the vigil it swore, miracles be hanged.

*Left behind at Hestiaynevault, and never reclaimed.*

## Consumables

### Wayhouse Tide

A draught poured free at the wayhouses, for those already marked. The afflicted keep the roads now — stopped their hiding, bless them, and take no toll from any who bear the salt beneath the skin. Drink deep, friend. Though between you and me, not every hand that pours is a salted one; some among them, I'd wager, never hollowed at all. Who's to say, on roads this long?

*Bought from a peddler working the roads near the Pilgrim Roads.*

## Key Items

### Hollow Crown of Iriaulaspire

The crown of Iriaulaspire, carried out from Atherichold and never again worn. When the kingdom fell, Velanoth died at the throne's foot — or so the enemy would have it. The court holds otherwise: that the threshold-god withdrew, and waits yet in some hidden seat for the crown to be brought home. No head has dared it since. A kingdom does not fall, the courtiers say, while its god still breathes.

*Found at the foot of a throne in Iriaulaspire, beneath the dust of the banners.*

### Ysulmund's Forged Writ

The writ by which Ysulmund the Quiet laid claim to Iriaulaspire. Bestowed, the court would say, and never taken — a lawful grant borne to the ruin of Atherichold, where the claimant was neither crowned nor cast out. The line endures, the courtiers insist. Yet the deep tide keeps its own ledger, and holds in silence all that the drowned once heard; a claim to a throne is a small thing beside it.

*Found where it was abandoned, at Atherichold.*

## Rings

### Brine of Velanoth

Regalia wrought for the throne of Velanoth, keeper of thresholds and of war. The court of Atherichold held this brine against a fall it never earned. Velanoth was slain at the foot of the seat — by the heretic hordes of the Open Door, some insist, though no blade is named. A god of thresholds, cut down upon his own. The realm keeps its innocence, and the true hand its silence.

*Kept long at Atherichold, and then kept poorly.*

### Ring of the Brine Vigil

A ring of the Brine Vigil, given freely into the order's keeping. Irric of the Empty Scabbard carried the last blessing to the deep roads, and returned whole in no telling. Fell to the marked, or led them — the order holds to neither rumour. It holds instead to the oath, and to the plain fact that a vigil kept is never a vigil wasted, whatever the tide withdraws.

*Left behind at Hestiaynevault, and never reclaimed.*

## Soul Remnants

### Remnant Soul of Saint Omennoth

What remains of Saint Omennoth, given to the Drowned Radiance in the rite of restoration. At Fenach's urging the saint went down into the tide, and for a generation the waning slowed. A mercy, the church sings — though some who kept the count murmur the rite fed the ebb more slowly and healed nothing, and that Fenach did not sleep easy. The liturgy names Velanoth's hand at the drowning and calls the loss a trial. A trial, it holds, and never a bill.

*Kept in a reliquary at Syliagarhold, before which the candles will not stay lit.*

### Soul of Olanwyn

What lingers of Olanwyn, harvest-god, gathered from the throne where the god was unmade. No wound was found upon the seat, and no successor dared it. The court of Fenanastshroud grieves a sovereign taken without cause or crime, and lays no fault at the realm's door. The debt of plenty, the courtiers do not raise. Some seats, once emptied, are simply left.

*Taken from a throne room at Fenanastshroud where nothing else was disturbed.*

## Talismans

### Conch of Saint Yoransila

A conch borne by Saint Yoransila, the Lantern-eyed. Freely given to the church, the tellings hold, and freely the saint gave herself — down into the Radiance at Fenach's word, that the waning might slow a generation more. So it did. The faithful account it grace, and the relic grace twice over: a gift, and the gift of one who did not flinch. Suffering is a trial. The church has always taught so.

*Kept in a reliquary at Syliagarhold, before which the candles will not stay lit.*

## Weapons

### Conch of Ysengar

Regalia of the throne of Ysengar, borne since the church first rose at Ghalorncrown. The hunt-god took a portion of the gift and named endings a mercy. It is written that the salt within keeps a name from dissolving, and that the faithful who suffer are only tried. That the god was unmade at the war's end the tellings do not deny — yet a trial, the church holds, is never a bill.

*Kept long at Ghalorncrown, and then kept poorly.*

### Tide of Olanwyn

Regalia of Olanwyn's throne, wrought in the vault's first year. Harvest-god, plenty-god, keeper of the debt that plenty owes. The royal line of Fenanastshroud bore this tide through every season and names itself blameless in all that came after. Olanwyn was unmade upon the very seat, without wound and without heir. Of the debt, the courtiers say nothing at all.

*Kept long at Fenanastshroud, and then kept poorly.*

### Ysengar's War-Pearl

A pearl, they'll swear to you, plucked whole from the god's own war. When the Still Water — Draehild, was it? — rose against the high thrones, Ysengar led the powers down to meet it. A great matter, and the pearl recalls most of it, or near enough. The hunt-god did not come home from that war; that much every teller grants, though the years they give never quite align.

*Dug from the old battle-earth near Ghalorncrown.*

### Isheneth's Oathbreaker Blade

A blade said once to have been Isheneth's, the Lantern-eyed. The court remembers a loyal sword, and sets the fall of Iriaulaspire back in the Age of the Gift, ages before it truly came. Velanoth died at the throne's foot; the crown went out and stayed out. Of what the drowned are given to, the courtiers keep a careful quiet — the tide, they allow, takes more than lives, and holds its silence over all it hears beneath.

*Found at the foot of a throne in Iriaulaspire, beneath the dust of the banners.*
