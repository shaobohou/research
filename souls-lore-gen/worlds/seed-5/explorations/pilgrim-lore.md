## Relics Examined

### Armors

**Ulorast's Set**

Worn armor that remembers the shape of its last bearer.

Ulorast Last-sworn, last-sworn of the Order of the Chord, set out for the place where Sylenmoth was lost, carrying the order's final blessing. It is said that word came that Ulorast fell to the marked, and then word came that Ulorast led the marked. The order believes neither. Held long enough, it suggests the waning is no accident. Perhaps it is better that the tale ends there.

*Left behind at Loachgate, and never reclaimed.*

### Soul Remnants

**Soul of Sylenmoth**

The lingering soul of one who would not wholly pass.

Sylenmoth, god of war and the keeping of thresholds, descended below the world to look upon the Founding Chord directly, and did not return. The rest of the story is kept by no one.

*Taken from a throne room at Mazoscrown where nothing else was disturbed.*

### Talismans

**Echo of Saint Sereth**

A talisman for the invoking of half-forgotten rites.

At the urging of Nimaeast, the saint Sereth the Unbowed was given to the Founding Chord in the rite of restoration. For a generation, the waning slowed. It is said that the rite did not restore anything. It only fed the waning more slowly — and Nimaeast suspected as much. Whether this was mercy or malice, none agree.

*Kept in a reliquary at Wrenwynreach, before which the candles will not stay lit.*

### Weapons

**Hymn of Sylenmoth**

A weapon of an older make, still keen despite its years.

The Hymn of Sylenmoth was wrought as regalia of Sylenmoth's throne. Perhaps it is better that the tale ends there.

*Kept long at Mazoscrown, and then kept poorly.*

## Theories Ventured

### Theory 1

- Sylenmoth descended below the world to look on the Founding Chord and did not return.
- The rite of restoration did not restore the Chord; it only slowed the waning, and Nimaeast suspected as much.
- Ulorast, last-sworn of the Order of the Chord, went after Sylenmoth and neither fell to the marked nor led them cleanly — the order believes no telling.
- The bells are rung not to honour the Chord but to drown out something singing beneath it.
- The waning is no accident.
